# Commercial Structure

## Commercial principle

The commercial model should reduce buyer risk while preserving Digital Heroes' ability to deliver meaningful outcomes. The best structure is a phased engagement that starts with diagnosis, moves into implementation, and expands into optimization once trust and measurable traction are established.

## Phased engagement

| Phase | Purpose | Scope | Relative pricing range | Decision gate |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| Phase 1 — Diagnostic and roadmap | Prove expertise and align stakeholders | Commercial audit, technical review, analytics review, stakeholder interviews, prioritized roadmap | 10%–15% of total first-program value | Approve roadmap and implementation scope. |
| Phase 2 — Foundation build | Remove core performance blockers | UX improvements, Shopify or web development fixes, tracking cleanup, landing page structure, QA process | 35%–45% of total first-program value | Confirm launch readiness and KPI baseline. |
| Phase 3 — Growth implementation | Turn improvements into revenue activity | Campaign landing pages, CRO tests, performance marketing support, reporting dashboards, iteration backlog | 30%–40% of total first-program value | Decide whether to move to retainer. |
| Phase 4 — Optimization retainer | Compound results and maintain momentum | Monthly experimentation, campaign support, site enhancements, executive reporting | Monthly retainer equal to 8%–15% of original implementation value | Renew quarterly based on performance and roadmap. |

## Scope per phase

### Phase 1 — Diagnostic and roadmap

* Executive discovery with COO and commercial stakeholders.
* Technical working session with the internal developer.
* Review of website, Shopify setup, analytics, campaign paths, conversion friction, and operational bottlenecks.
* Prioritized roadmap by impact, effort, risk, and dependency.
* Business case with recommended KPIs and governance model.

### Phase 2 — Foundation build

* High-priority site and storefront fixes.
* Tracking and reporting cleanup.
* Conversion-focused page improvements.
* Technical documentation and release process.
* QA and handover with the internal developer.

### Phase 3 — Growth implementation

* Landing page and campaign experience development.
* Conversion experiments and merchandising improvements.
* Performance marketing alignment and creative-to-page consistency.
* Weekly operating cadence and monthly executive readout.
* Backlog refinement based on early performance data.

### Phase 4 — Optimization retainer

* Monthly sprint planning.
* Ongoing CRO and landing page testing.
* Campaign launch support.
* Performance reporting and recommendations.
* Quarterly roadmap refresh.

## Relative pricing ranges

Because exact pricing depends on platform complexity, traffic volume, number of templates, analytics maturity, and stakeholder requirements, pricing should be presented as relative ranges rather than a fixed quote too early.

| Engagement type | Relative range | Use case |
|---|---:|---|
| Paid diagnostic | Low five figures or 10%–15% of total program | When the COO needs proof before approving implementation. |
| Focused implementation | Mid five figures | When there are defined, high-priority site and tracking improvements. |
| Full growth program | High five figures to low six figures | When Digital Heroes owns roadmap, development, campaign experience, and optimization. |
| Retainer | Low to mid five figures monthly | When ongoing experimentation and campaign support are required. |

## Upsell opportunities

| Upsell | Trigger | Business rationale |
|---|---|---|
| CRO experimentation retainer | Phase 2 improvements reveal new test opportunities | Converts implementation into continuous revenue improvement. |
| Performance marketing management | Buyer needs better traffic quality or creative-to-page alignment | Connects acquisition spend to conversion outcomes. |
| Shopify enhancement roadmap | Storefront requires ongoing merchandising and feature work | Keeps the internal team focused while Digital Heroes handles specialist execution. |
| Analytics and dashboarding | Leadership lacks reliable reporting | Supports COO-level decision making and budget confidence. |
| International or multi-brand rollout | Initial program proves repeatable | Expands value across regions, brands, or product lines. |

## Commercial reasoning

* A paid diagnostic avoids unpaid consulting and creates commitment before deep solution design.
* Phase gates make the deal easier for the COO and finance to approve.
* Implementation pricing reflects business value, complexity, and accountability rather than staff augmentation.
* A retainer should be positioned after value is demonstrated, not forced at the outset.
* Upsells should be tied to observed performance opportunities, not generic service bundling.
