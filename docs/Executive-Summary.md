# Executive Summary

## Recommendation

Digital Heroes should prioritize **growth-stage Shopify and DTC ecommerce brands in the United States, United Kingdom, and Australia with $3M–$30M annual online revenue**.

This segment has the clearest alignment with Digital Heroes' combined capabilities: Shopify Development, Web Development, and Performance Marketing. These buyers do not simply need a new website. They need a faster, higher-converting, better-tracked revenue engine that can support paid acquisition, retention, merchandising, and ongoing experimentation.

## Why this segment wins

| Evaluation factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Urgency | High. Conversion leaks, slow storefronts, poor attribution, and rising acquisition costs affect revenue immediately. |
| Budget fit | Strong. Brands at this stage can fund a serious build, optimization sprint, or monthly growth retainer. |
| Pain clarity | Strong. Problems are measurable through conversion rate, revenue per visitor, paid media efficiency, checkout abandonment, and site speed. |
| Digital Heroes fit | Excellent. Shopify, web development, and performance marketing can be sold as one commercial outcome. |
| Sales access | Good. Founders, heads of ecommerce, and marketing directors are visible and reachable through LinkedIn, ecommerce events, tech stacks, and ad libraries. |
| Expansion potential | High. Initial conversion or performance audit can expand into redesign, Shopify theme work, CRO, paid media, landing pages, analytics, and retention projects. |

## Strategic point of view

Digital Heroes should avoid positioning as a generalist development vendor. The agency should lead with a sharper commercial promise:

> We help growth-stage ecommerce teams turn traffic into profitable revenue through Shopify engineering, conversion-focused web experiences, and performance marketing execution.

This creates a stronger reason to buy than “we build websites” and avoids commoditized competition with freelance developers, low-cost offshore teams, and template-based agencies.

## Commercial motion

The recommended outbound motion is a **trigger-led, insight-based campaign** aimed at commercially accountable ecommerce leaders. The sequence should reference observable signals such as:

* paid traffic being sent to slow or generic product pages;
* visible merchandising or checkout friction;
* recent product launches or fundraising;
* migration signals from legacy storefronts;
* inconsistent messaging between ads, landing pages, and product detail pages.

The campaign should offer a low-friction diagnostic: a **15-minute revenue leak review** of the buyer's storefront, landing pages, and acquisition path.

## Funnel expectation

A realistic pilot campaign of **1,000 researched prospects** should produce approximately:

| Funnel stage | Volume | Conversion |
|---|---:|---:|
| Prospects | 1,000 | 100.0% |
| Replies | 90 | 9.0% |
| Meetings | 36 | 40.0% of replies |
| Qualified opportunities | 18 | 50.0% of meetings |
| Proposals | 12 | 66.7% of qualified opportunities |
| Closed won | 4 | 33.3% of proposals |

Assuming a conservative average first engagement value of **$35,000**, this creates **$140,000 in new project revenue** from one focused outbound campaign before retainers or expansion work.

## Qualification approach

The recommended framework is **SPICED** because it is discovery-led, customer-centric, and well suited to consultative agency sales. BANT is too procurement-oriented for early strategic conversations, while MEDDICC is powerful but heavier than necessary for most mid-market agency opportunities.

SPICED keeps the sales team focused on the commercial problem, measurable impact, decision process, urgency, and buying event. It also creates a practical bridge between initial discovery and a paid proposal.
